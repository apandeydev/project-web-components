Selected Ambient Works Volume II

Aphex Twin

1994

An almost entirely beatless double album — more texture than music, more feeling than structure. Each track is untitled, identified only by a photograph. It sounds like the inside of a dream you forgot.

Ambient Electronic Drone

What I Think

This album doesn't ask to be understood — it asks to be felt. I've listened to it during the quietest, most disorienting moments of my life. It's not background music; it's a different kind of foreground.
★★★★★

Lift Your Skinny Fists Like Antennas to Heaven

Godspeed You! Black Emperor

2000

A double album of four sprawling orchestral post-rock pieces — each building from silence to catastrophe and back. It feels like watching a civilization collapse and rise again in 87 minutes.

Post-Rock Orchestral Ambient

What I Think

Storm is the one. That build from the quiet piano into the full wall of strings — I've replayed that moment more times than I can count. It doesn't feel like music. It feels like grief becoming beautiful.
★★★★★

Favourite Tracks

01
Storm Starts quiet, ends like the world ending
02
Sleep 38 minutes of everything

A Love Supreme

John Coltrane

1965

A four-part suite — Acknowledgement, Resolution, Pursuance, Psalm — recorded as an offering to God. One of the most spiritual documents in the history of recorded music. The four-note motif alone changed jazz permanently.

Jazz Spiritual Suite

What I Think

Pursuance hits differently every time. There's a moment around the 4-minute mark where McCoy Tyner's chords drop out and it's just Coltrane and the drums — and that silence between the notes becomes the most important thing in the room.
★★★★★

Favourite Tracks

01
Pursuance When silence becomes the music
02
Acknowledgement The four-note motif that starts everything

The Disintegration Loops

William Basinski

2002

Old magnetic tapes played as they physically decayed — the music literally dying as it was recorded. Basinski watched the World Trade Center burn from his Brooklyn rooftop while this played. Time made audible.

Ambient Decay Electronic

What I Think

This is music about entropy. The loop starts almost beautiful, and you watch — or rather listen — as it slowly loses itself. By the end, you've witnessed something dying. I don't know how to describe that without it sounding morbid, because it isn't.
★★★★★
